# Quantification of class proportions, 2026-11-25

Script: `ml/quantify.py` (CPU only, no retraining), data in `quantify-2026-11-25.json`. Input: the
out-of-fold probabilities of A+xid (`oof_Axid.parquet`, end-of-block revision, folds and seed of the
baseline). Runtime 19 min.

## Central question and answer

*With a per-event accuracy of ~0.4-0.5, do PACC and EM estimate the class proportions of a set of
events well, and how much better than CC?*

- **Much better than CC whenever the class mix of the beam differs from the FI mix, but not "well"
  in absolute terms.** Physical scenario, N = 1000, mean over the 10 (workload, outcome) cells, total
  error TV (half the L1 distance = share of the mass assigned to the wrong class):
  CC 0.51, PACC 0.22, EM 0.17. CC is no better than not using the classifier at all (PRIOR, the FI
  class shares: 0.52). EM is 3x better than CC; ACC (hard matrix) 0.29 is clearly worse than PACC.
- **The error is bias, not variance.** EM goes from 0.21 (N = 100) to 0.18 (N = 300) to 0.17
  (N = 1000). More beam events do not fix it; the limit is the confusion matrix not transferring
  exactly between the calibration runs and the campaign runs (different runs on the same sites) and
  classes the model cannot separate.
- **Per cell, EM at N = 1000 ranges from 0.08 (gemm SDC) to 0.31 (cnn crash).** Good cells: gemm
  SDC 0.08, ptr_chase crash 0.07-0.09, cnn SDC 0.12, ptr_chase SDC 0.12, sfu_math SDC 0.13. Bad
  cells: cnn crash 0.31, shm_stencil SDC 0.30, sfu_math crash 0.25.
- **Dominant classes are estimated well; inseparable pairs are not.** mem_global in sfu_math SDC
  (true 0.50, AE 0.02), ld_data / mem_global in gemm SDC (0.01-0.03), mem_global in ptr_chase crash
  (0.96, AE 0.07). Errors concentrate on the known confusions: int / ld_const in cnn crash (AE
  0.26 / 0.16), addr / int in gemm and shm_stencil crashes (0.13-0.14), mem_shared in shm_stencil SDC
  (true 0.005, estimated ~0.17: mode-s shared runs look like the other SDCs of the stencil).
- **Uniform scenario: PCC looks best (0.20) but for the wrong reason.** The model is trained with
  balanced class weights, so its mean posterior is pulled towards uniform: PCC wins exactly when the
  truth is uniform and loses badly otherwise (physical 0.43). EM is the only method good in both
  (0.18 / 0.17).
- **Grouping does not help as done here.** Merging the most confused pairs until cond < 10 leaves 1-4
  groups per cell (25 in all). Every group with more than one class mixes propagation families (13 of
  13). The only "pure" groups are single classes: ur stays alone in 7 of 10 cells, mem_global in
  gemm SDC and sfu_math SDC. The warp/uniform family is identifiable; the others are not.
  Estimating directly on merged classes is often worse than estimating the fine classes and summing
  them into the groups (gemm SDC physical, EM: 0.03 summed vs 0.16 merged): a merged class has a
  feature distribution that depends on its internal mix, which differs between FI and beam, so the
  prior-shift assumption of PACC/EM breaks. Recommendation: estimate the fine classes, report
  group sums.
- **Bootstrap intervals of a single campaign are too narrow.** Nominal 95%, actual coverage
  0.33-0.89, worse at N = 1000 (0.33-0.80) than at N = 300 (0.59-0.89), because they only see the
  sampling noise of the events, not the bias of the matrix. A usable interval must also resample
  the calibration runs (matrix and calibrator), and probably the sites.

**Conclusion.** Stage 1 as a quantifier is usable for the large classes and for coarse shares
(memory vs registers, dominant functional unit), with errors of a few points; it is not usable
for fine shares of crash events, where TV is 0.15-0.31 whatever N is. CC must not be used. EM
on calibrated posteriors is the method of choice; PACC is a close and more robust second (no
calibrator, it was better on ptr_chase crash).

## Setup

- **Events.** Single-bit visible runs (SDC, crash/timeout) of the five small apps (gemm, cnn,
  ptr_chase, shm_stencil, sfu_math; the large variants are left out because the physical weights
  are for the small apps). SDC and crash/timeout are separate cells: the beam observes the outcome,
  so the proportions are conditional on it. Support of a cell = classes with >= 20 rows (the
  excluded classes and their physical mass are listed below; at most 6% of the mass).
- **Calibration vs campaign.** In each cell the rows are split at random, half of each class, into a
  calibration half (confusion matrices, calibrator, FI prior) and a pool; 20 such splits, cycled over
  the 200 campaigns. A campaign draws N events from the pool with replacement, with exactly
  round(N x proportion) events per class (largest remainder), so the true proportions are known
  exactly. The split is by run, not by site: the beam hits the same program, so the same static
  sites as the FI runs. The posteriors themselves are out-of-fold over site-grouped folds, so the
  classifier never saw the sites it scores (pessimistic for the classifier, as in the baseline).
  First attempt with calibration on the other folds abandoned: with site-grouped folds whole classes
  sit in one fold (e.g. pr of ptr_chase SDC: 949 of 974 rows in fold 1), so the matrix would miss them.
- **Estimators.**
  - PRIOR: the FI class shares of the calibration half (no classifier).
  - CC: argmax counts.
  - PCC: mean posterior.
  - ACC: hard confusion matrix inverted.
  - PACC: soft matrix (mean posterior per true class) inverted.
  - ACC/PACC solve a least-squares problem on the simplex (non-negative, summing to 1).
  - EM (Saerens, Latinne, Decaestecker 2002): EM re-weighting of posteriors calibrated by a
    multinomial logistic regression on the log-probabilities, fitted on the calibration half,
    starting from its class shares.
  - All posteriors are restricted to the support of the cell and renormalised.
- **Errors.** Absolute error per class and TV per campaign; mean and 2.5-97.5 percentiles over the
  200 campaigns (the "bootstrap intervals"). Separately, for PACC and EM in the physical scenario at
  N = 300 and 1000: 20 campaigns x 200 bootstrap resamples of the campaign events, coverage of the
  percentile interval.
- **Grouping.** On the soft matrix of all rows of the cell: merge the pair with the largest
  M_ij + M_ji until cond(M) < 10 (and < 30). Families: single value (fp32, fp16, fp64, tc, sfu,
  int, move, ld_data, ld_const), warp/uniform (ur), control flow (pr, up), pointer (addr), memory
  (mem_global, mem_shared). Before/after on the same campaigns: group-level TV of the fine estimates
  summed into the groups vs group-level TV of the estimators run on the merged classes.

## Physical weights

Weight of a class = exposed bits x liveness (steady state, time-averaged over the kernels, weighted by
their GPU duration, as in `fi_coverage/coverage_final.py`), then x P(outcome | class) of the FI runs
of the app (single bit, all outcomes including Masked), then normalised over the support of the cell.

| Structure | Bits | Source (coverage_inputs.json, exposure) |
|---|---|---|
| GPR | 20 SM x 64K x 32 bit x RF live share achieved | `rf_live_share_achieved` |
| PR | 20 SM x 48 warps x 32 lanes x 7 x occupancy x PR live of 7 | `warps_per_sm_achieved / 48`, `PR_live_of_7` |
| UR | 20 SM x 48 warps x 64 x 32 bit x 0.5 used x occupancy x UR live of used | `UR_live_of_used`; 0.5 = midpoint of the U(0.25, 0.75) of the coverage model |
| UP | 20 SM x 48 warps x 7 x occupancy x UP live of 7 | `UP_live_of_7` |
| global memory | 8 x peak live bytes | `global_memory.bytes_peak_live` |
| shared memory | live windows bit-s / run time | `shared_memory.gpu.exposure_bit_s.windows` (shm_stencil only) |

The GPR bits are split among the GPR classes (fp32, int, move, ld_data, ld_const, tc, sfu, fp16) by
the liveness-weighted label shares of the `gp` group runs (uniform over all GPR writers), and
addr takes a share f_addr = dynamic address uses / (address uses + GPR writes), from `group_instrs`
of the addr and gp groups (0.12-0.34). This is a proxy: the true share of live GPR bits that hold
an address is not measured.

Resulting class shares before the outcome factor:

| Workload | Largest shares |
|---|---|
| gemm | int 0.31, mem_global 0.24, addr 0.21, ld_data 0.18, ld_const 0.03, fp32 0.02, ur 0.01 |
| cnn | mem_global 0.53, ld_data 0.18, addr 0.13, int 0.07, move 0.05, ld_const 0.02, ur 0.01 |
| ptr_chase | mem_global 0.98 (4 MiB of nodes), the rest < 0.01 each |
| shm_stencil | mem_global 0.37, int 0.25, addr 0.21, ld_data 0.10, fp32 0.03, ur 0.02, mem_shared 0.003 |
| sfu_math | mem_global 0.45, int 0.21, ld_const 0.09, addr 0.06, ur 0.05, move 0.04, sfu 0.03 |

Caveats:
- pr, up and tc are almost absent physically, although they are large in the FI dataset.
- The shared-memory share of shm_stencil is tiny (live windows only: 0.06% of the SRAM bit-time).
- Global memory counts only the peak live bytes during kernels, not L2 or the time between kernels.

The physical scenario is therefore far from the FI mix, which is the realistic case and the one
where adjusted methods matter.

## Notes and limits

- **Same sites in calibration and campaign.** The split is by run, so a beam fault on a site never
  injected would be harder than measured here. In the other direction, the posteriors are
  site-out-of-fold, while a deployed model would be trained on all FI sites.
- **Small classes are reused.** Pools are sampled with replacement, and classes with 10-30 rows in
  the pool are reused many times. This narrows the spread between campaigns of the same cell but
  does not change the bias.
- **Ill-conditioned hard matrices.** They are singular in four cells (cond up to 1e19): some classes
  are never predicted. This is why ACC is unstable and PACC is preferred.
- **Excluded classes.** up, move and ld_const are excluded where they have fewer than 20 rows. Their
  physical mass is lost from the estimate (at most 0.06, cnn crash).
- **Assumptions in the physical weights.** They are listed above (f_addr proxy, UR used = 0.5,
  global memory = peak live bytes). Other assumptions would change the true proportions, not the
  ranking of the methods.

## Requirements for the Orin campaign (from this analysis)

15. **Calibration runs per (class, outcome).** At least ~100 visible FI runs per class and outcome
    in every workload, for the matrix and calibrator of PACC/EM. Classes below 20 rows here had to
    be dropped.
16. **Calibration runs held out from training.** A set of FI runs not used to train the network, on
    the same binaries as the beam, so that the matrix is estimated on predictions with the same
